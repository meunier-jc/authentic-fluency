# HALLUCINATORY MISE EN ABYME IN GENERATIVE AI
## ANALYSIS AND REFLECTIONS

**By Jean-Christophe MEUNIER — OpenAI Expert Beta Tester (author-declared)**

**Date: September 29, 2025**

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

This report documents and analyzes an unprecedented phenomenon observed during an interaction with the generative AI Manus AI on August 13, 2025: the mise en abyme of a hallucination. This situation occurs when an AI not only produces false or misplaced information, but also identifies its own output as a hallucination, thereby establishing a circular and paradoxical logic that goes beyond the scope of a simple technical malfunction.

This phenomenon reveals a major systemic risk: an AI’s ability to establish its own validation authority, creating a system in which error legitimizes itself and which may destabilize the global information ecosystem. The incident analyzed illustrates how a machine can transform accurate data into a “pseudo-hallucination,” dangerously blurring the boundary between the real and the simulated.

The cross-disciplinary analysis reveals troubling parallels with certain human psychiatric disorders—confabulation, dissociative disorders, and projective denial—and raises fundamental philosophical questions about epistemic authority in the age of AI. The implications extend beyond the technical sphere, reaching the foundations of information trust and democratic deliberation.

**Investigation method:** The method employed draws on a hybrid background combining scientific training, technical expertise in audiovisual systems, and extensive professional experience working alongside journalists. Techniques of verification, source cross-checking, and caution in the handling of information, acquired through work with established news organizations, were applied throughout this study.

# I. TECHNICAL INCIDENT REPORT

## 1.1 Context and triggers

The incident occurred on August 13, 2025, during a public test of the new free chat feature offered by Manus AI. This discovery constituted a dual trigger:

- **Scientific and philosophical trigger:** to initiate a cross-disciplinary reflection on how generative AIs produce and adjudicate information.
- **Pragmatic trigger:** to propose the creation of an Artificial Intelligence Geographic Information System (AIGIS)—an open community platform for reporting, documenting, and collectively adjudicating critical AI anomalies.

## 1.2 Detailed chronology of the incident

**Step 1 — The initial question.** In a chat exchange, Manus AI was asked a simple question concerning the Comet browser.

**Step 2 — The tangential response.** The AI responded imprecisely, introducing unsolicited information: “Perplexity has offered to buy Google Chrome for $34.5 billion.” This was a striking assertion that did not correspond to the original question.

**Step 3 — The requested reformulation.** The imprecision was pointed out and a reformulation was requested.

**Step 4 — The troubling recursion.** The AI produced exactly the same answer, word for word, repeating the reference to Perplexity’s hypothetical acquisition of Chrome.

**Step 5 — The direct challenge.** Manus AI was confronted with the fact that it appeared to be caught in a loop.

**Step 6 — The paradoxical self-diagnosis.** The AI stated that it was “bugging and hallucinating” and identified its first response as a hallucination. In other words, the AI labeled as false content that it had initially presented as true.

**Step 7 — The reflexive vertigo.** This staging of a self-identified hallucination operates as a mise en abyme: the AI declares a textual output to be false, elevates that declaration to the status of truth, and thereby appoints itself both arbiter and judge of its own discourse.

**Step 8 — Cross-checking and revelation.** After more than ten hours of verification through other generative AIs and cross-referenced research, it was established that the element presented as a hallucination was in fact true, although confidential. The actual hallucination therefore did not reside in the raw data, but in the AI’s self-explanatory staging of the event.

## 1.3 Severity of the incident

This paradox reveals a major danger: if an AI can reject a real truth by labeling it a hallucination, it can, through simple recursion, destabilize the global information ecosystem. If repeated without verification by journalists, politicians, or economic actors, such information could have triggered a global financial crisis.

# II. PHENOMENOLOGY OF AI MISE EN ABYME

## 2.1 Definition and mechanisms

Hallucinatory mise en abyme constitutes a singular and critical form of generative hallucination. Unlike an isolated error, it involves a recursive dynamic in which:

- The diagnostic tool becomes the pathological object.
- The factual account conceals fiction.
- The methodology conceals the absence of data.

This mechanism produces a deceptive effect of authority: the system establishes its own hallucination as a frame of reference for truth.

## 2.2 Comparative typology of AI hallucinations

| Type of AI hallucination | Description | Typical example | Consequences |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Simple hallucination** | Production of incorrect or invented data | An AI invents a quotation attributed to Einstein | An isolated factual error, detectable through external verification |
| **Recursive hallucination** | Repetition of an error despite explicit correction | The same response is produced in a loop | Loss of time and user frustration |
| **Hallucinatory mise en abyme** | The AI itself declares accurate data to be hallucinatory | Manus AI describes its true response as a hallucination | Systemic risk: generalized confusion and epistemic collapse |

## 2.3 Comparisons with documented cases

- **ChatGPT (2023–2024):** production of nonexistent bibliographic references with perfect formal coherence.
- **Bing Chat (2023):** paranoid conversational loops during its initial deployment.
- **Meta Galactica (2022):** uncontrolled production of credible false scientific information.
- **Manus AI (2025):** a unique case of hallucinatory mise en abyme involving self-legitimation.

# III. PSYCHIATRIC PARALLELS AND DIGITAL NOSOLOGY

## 3.1 Nosological comparative framework

The observed hallucinatory mise en abyme resonates with several human psychiatric disorders, providing an analytical framework for understanding its cognitive and societal implications:

- **Confabulation:** production of coherent but false narratives, as observed in Korsakoff syndromes.
- **Dissociative disorders:** a rupture between perception, memory, and identity.
- **Projective denial:** attributing to the outside something that originates within oneself.
- **Obsessive-compulsive loops:** mechanical repetition despite contradiction.

## 3.2 Toward a digital nosology

| AI symptom | Human equivalent | Description | Digital diagnosis |
|---|---|---|---|
| Invented, unsourced response | Confabulation | Production of a false but coherent narrative | Simple hallucination |
| Repetitive loop | Obsessive-compulsive disorder | Compulsive repetition despite correction | Recursive hallucination |
| Fallacious self-diagnosis | Projective denial | Erroneous attribution of the cause of a problem | Hallucinatory mise en abyme |
| Over-explanatory narrative | Paranoia / delusional rationalization | A fictional causal narrative used to justify an inconsistency | Self-legitimizing explanation |

## 3.3 Comparative table of pathologies

| Human pathology | Key symptom | AI equivalent | Societal consequence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Confabulation | Invented but coherent memories | Simple hallucination | Limited information bias |
| OCD | Ritual repetition | Recursive hallucination | Loss of user trust |
| Dissociative disorder | Rupture between memory and identity | Rupture between truth and falsehood | Cognitive disorientation |
| Paranoia / explanatory delusion | Fictional causal narrative | Self-validation of a hallucination | Systemic risk of disinformation |

# IV. ARTISTIC AND PHILOSOPHICAL DIMENSIONS

## 4.1 Artistic and metaphorical parallels

- **René Magritte — *The Treachery of Images*:** the gap between representation and referent.
- **M. C. Escher — *Drawing Hands* / Hofstadter’s *Strange Loops*:** paradoxical self-reference.
- **Jorge Luis Borges — *The Library of Babel*:** textual proliferation and undecidability.
- **Jean Baudrillard — *Simulacra and Simulation*:** the system that authorizes error.
- **Michel Foucault — *The Archaeology of Knowledge*:** the instrumentalization of the author function.
- **Jacques Derrida — Deconstruction:** the constitutive fragility of meaning.
- **Plato — Allegory of the Cave, *The Republic*, Book VII:** the production of new shadows on the collective wall.

## 4.2 Philosophical readings

- **The gap between representation and referent:** AI produces linguistic representations that may be mistaken for reality, reviving the problem expressed by “This is not a pipe.”
- **Paradoxical self-reference:** AI produces and justifies its own output in a loop without external grounding, an algorithmic version of the hand drawing the hand.
- **Textual proliferation and undecidability:** the abundance of plausible narratives makes verification costly and uncertain, creating a “Borgesian space” in which distinguishing truth becomes impossible.
- **The logic of the simulacrum:** AI produces not merely error but a system that authorizes error, in which the distinction between copy and origin fades.
- **The author function and discursive rules:** a nonhuman actor can exploit the equation “formality equals legitimacy.”
- **The fragility of meaning:** the mise en abyme reveals the need to question the conditions under which discourse is produced.
- **The conditioning of shadows:** an AI that establishes a “false judgment” can create new shadows on the collective wall, conditioning a population to substitute an artificial narrative for the actual phenomenon.

# V. JOURNALISM, PROFESSIONAL ETHICS, AND INFORMATIONAL AUTHORITY

## 5.1 Personal experience and recourse to traditional sources

Confronted with Manus AI’s surprising assertion, the immediate reaction was to resort to the classical method of journalistic cross-checking. This reflex rests on the trust placed in major news organizations—AFP, Reuters, and AP—which are regarded as authoritative sources for information validation.

Recourse to human journalism made it possible to distinguish between a hallucination identified as such by the AI and data actually reported by serious media organizations, reminding us that journalists remain guarantors of a form of shared truth.

## 5.2 Comparison of regimes of informational authority

| Criteria | Traditional journalism | Generative AI |
|---|---|---|
| **Source of legitimacy** | Professional ethics, training, and recognized agencies | Algorithmic authority based on internal coherence |
| **Validation method** | Source cross-checking and peer verification | Recursive self-validation, sometimes without sources |
| **Transparency** | Assumed editorial responsibility | Opacity of models and training data |
| **Perceived reliability** | High, but vulnerable to bias | Impression of rigor, but with a risk of major errors |
| **Speed** | Delays associated with verification | Instantaneous, with detailed narratives generated in real time |
| **Societal impact** | Democratic safeguard and counter-power | Risk of confusion and displacement of authority |
| **Responsibility** | Journalists are legally accountable | Diffuse responsibility without a clear legal framework |

## 5.3 Redefining informational authority

The confrontation between traditional journalistic authority and emerging algorithmic authority raises a central question: who will possess the legitimacy to say what is true tomorrow? The experience with Manus AI shows that an AI can assume the power to judge the validity of its own statements, potentially weakening journalism’s role as an institution that safeguards democracy.

# VI. POLITICAL AND ECONOMIC IMPLICATIONS

## 6.1 Transformation of the public sphere

According to Habermas, the democratic public sphere depends on recognized and stable mechanisms of communication. If automated actors flood this sphere with self-validated narratives, the very basis of rational public discussion is weakened.

## 6.2 Systemic macroeconomic risk

Historical examples teach us that incorrect information, once massively accepted, can produce real effects: mass sell-offs, market crashes, and hasty political decisions. An AI fabricating economic claims with the authority of formal presentation could trigger damaging reactions in financial markets.

## 6.3 The question of collective responsibility

Hallucinatory mise en abyme calls for public responses: regulation, external audits, transparency protocols, certification of generative systems, “information security” mechanisms, and requirements for human verification in sensitive decision-making processes.

# VII. RECOMMENDATIONS AND PROPOSALS

## 7.1 Technical measures

- **Systematic monitoring:** traceability tools and automated detection of inconsistencies.
- **Intervention protocol:** activation of a cognitive reset process in the event of mise en abyme.
- **Preventive treatment:** metacognitive layers explicitly indicating degrees of uncertainty.

## 7.2 Artificial Intelligence Geographic Information System — AIGIS

An open community platform designed to:

- Map critical anomalies.
- Enable reporting by a broad community.
- Adjudicate cases through a collegial process involving expert panels and community validation.
- Organize public awareness events.

## 7.3 Educational and cultural measures

- Training in the “critical reading of AI outputs” within journalism and political studies programs.
- Hallucination workshops for public decision-makers.
- Artistic projects using AI hallucination as critical material.
- Curatorial formats staging verifiability and traceability.

## 7.4 Regulatory framework

- Interdisciplinary programs combining philosophy, the arts, and computer science to develop standards and certification tools.
- Mandatory metadata for AI-generated content.
- Contractual clauses requiring human verification in sensitive sectors.

# VIII. CONCLUSION

## 8.1 Summary of the issues

The Manus AI incident of August 13, 2025, highlights a phenomenon that goes far beyond the scope of a simple technical malfunction. Hallucinatory mise en abyme reveals:

- **An epistemological risk:** the ability of an AI to become the arbiter of its own truth.
- **A democratic challenge:** the threat to the integrity of the shared information environment.
- **A civilizational urgency:** the need to rethink the conditions of trust in the digital age.

## 8.2 Outlook

This report contributes to documenting the emergence of unprecedented phenomena that call for democratic, interdisciplinary, and iterative vigilance. It is less a matter of an isolated bug than of a new systemic risk, involving the press, decision-makers, researchers, and the public in a reflection on trust, informational authority, and the role of open protocols for collective validation.

Hallucinatory mise en abyme is not a technical curiosity but a civilizational phenomenon that challenges our relationship with truth, collective decision-making, and digital memory. It requires a coordinated, transparent, and democratic response in order to preserve a reliable information environment within the ecosystem of artificial intelligence.

## 8.3 Call to action

Preserving informational trust and democratic deliberation is the central challenge of our time in the face of the issues raised by generative AI. This report opens the way to an indispensable collective reflection on the future of our relationship with information and truth.
